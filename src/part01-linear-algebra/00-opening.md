# Part I — The Grammar of Computation

The prologue showed the copper wire at every scale — continuum, dislocation, atom, electron. Before we climb that ladder, we need a shared language. At every rung, no matter how exotic the physics, computation eventually reduces to **finite-dimensional algebra**: states become vectors, evolution becomes matrix multiplication, stability becomes an eigenvalue sign.

This part refreshes linear algebra with mechanics in mind. Vectors are nodal displacements; matrices are stiffness operators; eigenvalues are vibration modes. The final chapter takes the decisive step from \(\mathbb{R}^N\) toward function spaces: inner products become integrals, matrices become operators, and the mesh size \(h\) becomes the parameter that sends us toward infinite dimensions.

The layout follows the **Linear Algebra Notes** in [`writings/linear-algebra/`](../../writings/linear-algebra/): numbered chapters, worked examples on the copper wire, and a **Bridge** at the end of each chapter. Read the four chapters in order; Chapter 04 hands off directly to Part II, where the limit \(N \to \infty\) is made precise.
